<h2 id="problem">Problem</h2>

<div className="problem-statement">
  <span className="problem-label">Reorganizing perturbation theory</span>
  <p>
    Starting from the old-fashioned Born expansion, whose intermediate states carry denominators $(E_\alpha-E_\gamma+i0)^{-1}$, derive the ordered interaction-time expansion of the $S$-operator directly.
  </p>
</div>

<details className="problem-hint">
  <summary>Hint</summary>
  <p>
    Write each denominator as an integral over a positive time interval. Use the energy-conserving delta function to introduce one unrestricted reference time, then replace the positive intervals by a descending sequence $t_1\ge t_2\ge\cdots\ge t_n$.
  </p>
</details>

<p>
  This solution supplies the algebra behind <Link to={scatteringTheoryProblems[3].theoryRoute}>the perturbative bridge to the Dyson series</Link>.
</p>

<Callout title="One expansion in two languages">
Energy denominators emphasize the free intermediate states through which the system propagates. Ordered interaction times emphasize when successive insertions occur. The derivation below is a change of representation, not a new dynamical assumption.
</Callout>

<h2 id="born-term">1. Isolate the general Born contribution</h2>

Let $H=H_0+V$, with

$$
H_0|\Phi_\alpha\rangle
=
E_\alpha|\Phi_\alpha\rangle.
$$

At order $n$, the incoming Lippmann-Schwinger kernel contains $n$ insertions of $V$ and $n-1$ free resolvents:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\beta\alpha}^{(n)}
&=
\int d\gamma_1\cdots d\gamma_{n-1}
\\[2pt]
&\quad\times
\frac{
V_{\beta\gamma_{n-1}}
V_{\gamma_{n-1}\gamma_{n-2}}
\cdots
V_{\gamma_1\alpha}
}{
\prod_{r=1}^{n-1}
(E_\alpha-E_{\gamma_r}+i0)
}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The associated connected S-matrix contribution in this energy-normalized Hamiltonian basis is

$$
S_{\beta\alpha}^{(n)}
=
-2\pi i\,
\delta(E_\beta-E_\alpha)
\tau_{\beta\alpha}^{(n)}.
$$

This relation is compatible with the notebook's $S=1+i\mathcal T$: it says that the Hamiltonian kernel is packaged into

$$
\langle\beta|\mathcal T|\alpha\rangle
=
-2\pi\delta(E_\beta-E_\alpha)
\tau_{\beta\alpha}^+.
$$

<h2 id="fourier">2. Replace every energy factor by a time integral</h2>

The outgoing-wave prescription has the Fourier representation

$$
\boxed{
\frac{1}{E_\alpha-E_\gamma+i0}
=
-i\int_0^\infty d\tau\,
e^{i(E_\alpha-E_\gamma)\tau}.
}
$$

The small positive imaginary part supplies the convergence factor $e^{-0^+\tau}$. It is not optional.

The energy-conserving delta function is

$$
2\pi\delta(E_\beta-E_\alpha)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1\,
e^{i(E_\beta-E_\alpha)t_1}.
$$

There is one factor $-i$ from the S-matrix relation and one from each of the $n-1$ resolvents. The coefficient of the order-$n$ term is therefore

$$
(-i)^n.
$$

At this stage, $t_1$ ranges over the whole real line, while every $\tau_r$ is nonnegative.

<h2 id="ordered-times">3. Convert intervals into ordered interaction times</h2>

Use the positive intervals to define earlier vertex times. With the intermediate states ordered from earliest to latest, set

$$
t_2=t_1-\tau_{n-1},
$$

$$
t_3=t_2-\tau_{n-2},
$$

and continue until $t_n$. The Jacobian of this triangular change of variables is one. Since every $\tau_r\ge0$,

$$
\boxed{t_1\ge t_2\ge\cdots\ge t_n.}
$$

Now introduce the interaction-picture operator

$$
V_I(t)
=
e^{iH_0t}Ve^{-iH_0t}.
$$

Between free energy eigenstates,

$$
\langle\Phi_a|V_I(t)|\Phi_b\rangle
=
e^{i(E_a-E_b)t}V_{ab}.
$$

The exponentials generated by the Fourier integrals therefore combine as

$$
\begin{aligned}
&e^{i(E_\beta-E_{\gamma_{n-1}})t_1}
e^{i(E_{\gamma_{n-1}}-E_{\gamma_{n-2}})t_2}
\cdots
e^{i(E_{\gamma_1}-E_\alpha)t_n}
\\
&\qquad\times
V_{\beta\gamma_{n-1}}
V_{\gamma_{n-1}\gamma_{n-2}}
\cdots
V_{\gamma_1\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Insert completeness at each intermediate label. The whole expression becomes

$$
\langle\Phi_\beta|
V_I(t_1)V_I(t_2)\cdots V_I(t_n)
|\Phi_\alpha\rangle.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
S_{\beta\alpha}^{(n)}
&=
(-i)^n
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1
\int_{-\infty}^{t_1}dt_2
\cdots
\int_{-\infty}^{t_{n-1}}dt_n
\\
&\quad\times
\langle\Phi_\beta|
V_I(t_1)V_I(t_2)\cdots V_I(t_n)
|\Phi_\alpha\rangle.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

<h2 id="dyson">4. Sum all orders</h2>

Since the relation holds between arbitrary free states, it is an operator identity:

$$
\boxed{
S
=
1+
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-i)^n
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1
\int_{-\infty}^{t_1}dt_2
\cdots
\int_{-\infty}^{t_{n-1}}dt_n
V_I(t_1)\cdots V_I(t_n).
}
$$

The nested limits already impose chronological ordering, with the latest time on the left. If instead every time is integrated independently over the whole real line, all $n!$ orderings occur equally. Therefore

$$
\boxed{
S
=
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1\cdots dt_n\,
\mathrm T\{V_I(t_1)\cdots V_I(t_n)\}.
}
$$

This is written compactly as

$$
\boxed{
S
=
\mathrm T\exp\!\left[-i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,V_I(t)\right].
}
$$

<h2 id="second-order">5. Check the map at second order</h2>

The second Born term is

$$
S_{\beta\alpha}^{(2)}
=
-2\pi i\delta(E_\beta-E_\alpha)
\int d\gamma\,
\frac{V_{\beta\gamma}V_{\gamma\alpha}}
{E_\alpha-E_\gamma+i0}.
$$

After the two Fourier representations,

$$
S_{\beta\alpha}^{(2)}
=
(-i)^2
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1
\int_0^\infty d\tau
\int d\gamma\,
e^{i(E_\beta-E_\alpha)t_1}
e^{i(E_\alpha-E_\gamma)\tau}
V_{\beta\gamma}V_{\gamma\alpha}.
$$

Set $t_2=t_1-\tau$. Then $t_2\le t_1$, and the exponent becomes

$$
(E_\beta-E_\gamma)t_1
+(E_\gamma-E_\alpha)t_2.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{
S_{\beta\alpha}^{(2)}
=
(-i)^2
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt_1
\int_{-\infty}^{t_1}dt_2\,
\langle\Phi_\beta|V_I(t_1)V_I(t_2)|\Phi_\alpha\rangle.
}
$$

This explicit case contains the entire logic of the general proof.

<div className="answer-check">
  <strong>Answer check</strong>
  <p>
    At first order the result is $-i\int dt\,V_I(t)$. At second order it is $(-i)^2$ integrated over exactly one of the two time-ordering triangles. These are the first two nontrivial Dyson terms with the correct signs and no extra combinatorial factor.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 id="interpretation">Interpretation and common traps</h2>

An old-fashioned denominator records how a free intermediate state fails to conserve the external energy. Its Fourier transform says that the same intermediate state propagates for a positive interval between two interaction events. Time ordering is therefore already encoded in the $+i0$ prescription.

Common traps are:

1. Reversing the sign in the Fourier representation of $(x+i0)^{-1}$.
2. Forgetting that $i0$ supplies convergence at large positive interval.
3. Adding $1/n!$ while retaining nested ordered integrals. That would divide each ordering twice.
4. Assuming the intermediate labels are observed particles. They are summed free-basis states inside a perturbative representation.
5. Thinking the Dyson series proves convergence. It reorganizes the expansion; it does not change its generally asymptotic character.

<h2 id="checkpoint">Checkpoint</h2>

The dictionary is

$$
\text{energy denominator}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{positive propagation interval},
$$

$$
\text{energy delta function}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{one unrestricted reference time},
$$

$$
\text{Born chain}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{chronologically ordered interaction-picture product}.
$$
