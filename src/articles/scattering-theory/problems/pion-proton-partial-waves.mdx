<h2 id="channels">1. Identify the symmetry eigenchannels</h2>

The pion has spin zero and intrinsic parity $-1$. The proton has spin $\tfrac12$ and intrinsic parity $+1$. Coupling orbital angular momentum $l$ to the proton spin gives

$$
J=l+\frac12,
$$

or, for $l\ge1$,

$$
J=l-\frac12.
$$

It is conventional to denote these alternatives by $l+$ and $l-$.

The two-particle parity is

$$
P
=
P_\pi P_p(-1)^l
=
(-1)^{l+1}.
$$

For fixed $l$, the two values of $J$ have the same parity. Conversely, at fixed $J$, the two possible orbital angular momenta have opposite parity, so parity conservation prevents them from mixing.

The pion carries isospin $1$ and the nucleon isospin $\tfrac12$. Therefore

$$
1\otimes\frac12
=
\frac32\oplus\frac12.
$$

The strong-interaction eigenchannels may be labelled

$$
(I,l,J,P)
=
\left(I,l,l\pm\frac12,(-1)^{l+1}\right),
$$

with $I=\tfrac12$ or $\tfrac32$.

<Callout title="Why the phase shifts may be taken real">
Below the first genuinely inelastic threshold, each fixed $(I,l,J,P)$ channel is elastic. Unitarity then puts its S-matrix eigenvalue on the unit circle. Once extra-particle channels open, an inelasticity parameter must accompany the phase shift.
</Callout>

<h2 id="eigenamplitudes">2. Define each partial-wave amplitude</h2>

For an elastic eigenchannel, write

$$
S_{l\pm}^{I}
=
e^{2i\delta_{l\pm}^{I}},
$$

where $\delta_{l\pm}^{I}$ is real. With center-of-mass momentum $k$, define

$$
a_{l\pm}^{I}
=
\frac{S_{l\pm}^{I}-1}{2ik}.
$$

Using $e^{2i\delta}-1=2ie^{i\delta}\sin\delta$,

$$
\boxed{
a_{l\pm}^{I}
=
\frac{e^{i\delta_{l\pm}^{I}}\sin\delta_{l\pm}^{I}}{k}.
}
$$

This convention is aligned with the notebook's

$$
S_l=1+2ik\,a_l=e^{2i\delta_l}.
$$

If the source amplitude is used instead, its overall sign differs because its connected S-matrix is written with $-2\pi i$ rather than the notebook's $+i\mathcal T$. The phase-shift formula above avoids that ambiguity by defining the physical S-matrix eigenvalue first.

<h2 id="spin-amplitudes">3. Assemble non-spin-flip and spin-flip motion</h2>

Rotational invariance allows the scattering operator in proton spin space to contain two structures:

$$
\mathcal F^I(\theta)
=
F^I(\theta)
+i(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\widehat{\mathbf n})G^I(\theta),
$$

where

$$
\widehat{\mathbf n}
=
\frac{\mathbf k'\times\mathbf k}
{|\mathbf k'\times\mathbf k|}
$$

is normal to the scattering plane. $F^I$ preserves a spin projection defined relative to that plane, while $G^I$ supplies the spin-dependent rotation.

Coupling the orbital harmonics to spin $\tfrac12$ gives

$$
\boxed{
F^I(\theta)
=
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}
\left[(l+1)a_{l+}^{I}+l\,a_{l-}^{I}\right]
P_l(\cos\theta).
}
$$

The coefficient $l+1$ counts the contribution of $J=l+\tfrac12$, whereas $l$ accompanies $J=l-\tfrac12$. The spin-flip amplitude is

$$
\boxed{
G^I(\theta)
=
\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}
\left[a_{l+}^{I}-a_{l-}^{I}\right]
P_l^1(\cos\theta).
}
$$

We use the Condon-Shortley convention

$$
P_l^1(x)
=
-\sqrt{1-x^2}\,P_l'(x).
$$

Changing the orientation of $\widehat{\mathbf n}$ or the sign convention for $P_l^1$ reverses $G^I$, but it cannot change an unpolarized cross-section.

Indeed, average over the initial proton spin and sum over the final spin:

$$
\frac12\operatorname{tr}
\left(\mathcal F^I\mathcal F^{I\dagger}\right)
=
|F^I|^2+|G^I|^2.
$$

The interference term vanishes because $\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol\sigma=0$.

<h2 id="charge-states">4. Express the charged states in the isospin basis</h2>

The state $|\pi^+p\rangle$ has total third component

$$
I_3=1+\frac12=\frac32.
$$

Only an $I=\tfrac32$ multiplet contains that value, so

$$
\boxed{
|\pi^+p\rangle
=
\left|\frac32,\frac32\right\rangle.
}
$$

The $\pi^-p$ state has $I_3=-\tfrac12$ and can belong to either total isospin. With one standard phase convention,

$$
\boxed{
|\pi^-p\rangle
=
\sqrt{\frac13}
\left|\frac32,-\frac12\right\rangle
+
\sqrt{\frac23}
\left|\frac12,-\frac12\right\rangle.
}
$$

Strong scattering is diagonal in $I$. Therefore an elastic matrix element contains the squares of the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients:

$$
F_{\pi^-p}
=
\frac13F^{3/2}+\frac23F^{1/2},
$$

$$
G_{\pi^-p}
=
\frac13G^{3/2}+\frac23G^{1/2}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{
F_{\pi^-p}
=
\frac{F^{3/2}+2F^{1/2}}{3},
\qquad
G_{\pi^-p}
=
\frac{G^{3/2}+2G^{1/2}}{3}.
}
$$

These are coherent amplitudes. The two total-isospin possibilities are not separately observed in an elastic charged-particle measurement.

<h2 id="cross-sections">5. Write the physical differential cross-sections</h2>

For $\pi^+p$, the pure-isospin result is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\sigma(\pi^+p\to\pi^+p)}{d\Omega}
=
|F^{3/2}(\theta)|^2
+|G^{3/2}(\theta)|^2.
}
$$

For $\pi^-p$,

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\sigma(\pi^-p\to\pi^-p)}{d\Omega}
&=
\left|
\frac{F^{3/2}(\theta)+2F^{1/2}(\theta)}{3}
\right|^2
\\
&\quad+
\left|
\frac{G^{3/2}(\theta)+2G^{1/2}(\theta)}{3}
\right|^2.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

Every $F^I$ and $G^I$ on the right is itself the partial-wave sum built from the definite-$(I,J,P)$ phase shifts $\delta_{l\pm}^I$.

As a consistency check, the same Clebsch-Gordan algebra predicts the charge-exchange amplitude

$$
\mathcal F(\pi^-p\to\pi^0n)
\propto
\frac{\sqrt2}{3}
\left(\mathcal F^{3/2}-\mathcal F^{1/2}\right),
$$

up to the phase chosen for the charge states. If the two isospin amplitudes were equal, charge exchange would disappear, as it should for an interaction blind to total isospin.

<div className="answer-check">
  <strong>Answer check</strong>
  <p>
    If all phase shifts vanish, then every $a_{l\pm}^{I}$ vanishes and so do both cross-sections. If only an $s$ wave is present, $G^I=0$ and $F^I=a_{0+}^{I}$ is isotropic. Both limits agree with angular-momentum expectations.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 id="interpretation">Interpretation and common traps</h2>

The calculation separates three layers. Rotations decide which $J$ values occur and how their angular functions combine. Parity prevents the two orbital possibilities at fixed $J$ from mixing. Isospin determines how the physical charge channels sample the two strong-interaction eigenamplitudes.

Common traps are:

1. Adding $I=\tfrac12$ and $I=\tfrac32$ probabilities for $\pi^-p$. The amplitudes are coherent and must be combined before squaring.
2. Forgetting that $\pi^+p$ is pure $I=\tfrac32$.
3. Omitting the spin-flip amplitude $G$ when $l\ge1$.
4. Assigning parity as $(-1)^l$ and forgetting the pion's negative intrinsic parity.
5. Treating the sign of $G$ as physical without specifying both $\widehat{\mathbf n}$ and the associated-Legendre convention.
6. Using real phase shifts above inelastic thresholds without an inelasticity parameter.

<h2 id="checkpoint">Checkpoint</h2>

The symmetry labels are

$$
I\in\left\{\frac12,\frac32\right\},
\qquad
J=l\pm\frac12,
\qquad
P=(-1)^{l+1}.
$$

They enter through $S_{l\pm}^I=e^{2i\delta_{l\pm}^I}$, first building $F^I$ and $G^I$, then the physical $\pi^+p$ or $\pi^-p$ amplitude, and only at the end the cross-section.
