<h2 id="kinematics">1. Set up laboratory kinematics</h2>

Write

$$
p_1=(E_1,\mathbf p),
\qquad
p_2=(m_2,\mathbf0),
$$

with

$$
p=|\mathbf p|,
\qquad
E_1=\sqrt{p^2+m_1^2}.
$$

Let the observed particle be

$$
p_3=(E_3,\mathbf q),
\qquad
q=|\mathbf q|,
\qquad
\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q=pq\cos\theta,
$$

where

$$
E_3(q)=\sqrt{q^2+m_3^2}.
$$

Momentum conservation makes the unobserved recoil momentum

$$
\mathbf p_4=\mathbf p-\mathbf q,
$$

so

$$
E_4(q,\theta)
=
\sqrt{p^2+q^2-2pq\cos\theta+m_4^2}.
$$

The allowed value of $q$ is not independent. It is fixed by

$$
\boxed{
E_1+m_2=E_3(q)+E_4(q,\theta).
}
$$

For ordinary kinematics there is one physical positive root. In more general situations, the final answer must sum over all physical roots.

<h2 id="master-formula">2. Start from the invariant master formula</h2>

With

$$
\langle\mathbf p',\sigma'|\mathbf p,\sigma\rangle
=
2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3
\delta^3(\mathbf p'-\mathbf p)\delta_{\sigma'\sigma},
$$

and

$$
\langle f|i\mathcal T|i\rangle
=
i(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P_f-P_i)\mathcal M_{fi},
$$

the cross-section is

$$
d\sigma
=
\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}
{4\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}}
(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_1+p_2-p_3-p_4)
\prod_{r=3}^{4}
\frac{d^3p_r}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_r}.
$$

The bar denotes the desired average over initial discrete labels and sum over final ones. If those labels are fixed instead, omit the average.

In the target rest frame,

$$
p_1\cdot p_2=E_1m_2.
$$

Because $E_1^2-m_1^2=p^2$,

$$
\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}
=
m_2p.
$$

The invariant flux denominator is therefore

$$
\boxed{4m_2p.}
$$

<Callout title="The flux is already relativistic">
One may also write the denominator as $4E_1m_2v_{\rm rel}$. In the target rest frame $v_{\rm rel}=p/E_1$, giving the same $4m_2p$. No separate velocity factor should be added later.
</Callout>

<h2 id="momentum-delta">3. Eliminate the recoil momentum</h2>

Use the three-dimensional delta function to perform the $d^3p_4$ integral. Then write

$$
d^3p_3=q^2\,dq\,d\Omega.
$$

The factors of $2\pi$ and the one-particle energies give

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
=
\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}
{64\pi^2m_2p}
\int_0^\infty dq\,
\frac{q^2}{E_3E_4}
\delta\!\left(E_1+m_2-E_3-E_4\right).
$$

Up to this point, momentum conservation has determined the recoil direction and magnitude, but energy conservation still has to select the physical $q$.

<h2 id="jacobian">4. Evaluate the energy-delta Jacobian</h2>

Define

$$
f(q)=E_1+m_2-E_3(q)-E_4(q,\theta).
$$

Its derivative is

$$
f'(q)
=
-\frac{q}{E_3}
-\frac{q-p\cos\theta}{E_4}.
$$

The delta-function identity

$$
\delta(f(q))
=
\sum_i\frac{\delta(q-q_i)}{|f'(q_i)|}
$$

therefore gives

$$
\frac{q_i^2}{E_3E_4|f'(q_i)|}
=
\frac{q_i^2}
{\left|q_iE_4+(q_i-p\cos\theta)E_3\right|}.
$$

The laboratory differential cross-section is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega_{\rm lab}}
=
\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}
{64\pi^2m_2p}
\sum_i
\frac{q_i^2}
{\left|q_iE_4+(q_i-p\cos\theta)E_3\right|}.
}
$$

Energy conservation also lets us write $E_3+E_4=E_1+m_2$. Hence an equivalent compact form is

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega_{\rm lab}}
=
\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}
{64\pi^2m_2p}
\sum_i
\frac{q_i^2}
{\left|q_i(E_1+m_2)-pE_3\cos\theta\right|}.
}
$$

All energies in the denominator are evaluated at the corresponding physical root $q_i$.

<h2 id="source-convention">5. Translate the older state normalization</h2>

The source exercise denotes by $M_{\beta\alpha}^{\rm src}$ an amplitude between states normalized as $\delta^3(\mathbf p'-\mathbf p)$ and writes its connected S-matrix with a factor $-2\pi i\delta^4(P_f-P_i)$. For four external particles,

$$
\mathcal M
=
-4(2\pi)^3
\sqrt{E_1E_2E_3E_4},
M_{\beta\alpha}^{\rm src}.
$$

Here $E_2=m_2$. Substitution into the boxed covariant result gives the literal source-normalized form

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega_{\rm lab}}
=
(2\pi)^4\frac{E_1}{p}
|M_{\beta\alpha}^{\rm src}|^2
\sum_i
\frac{q_i^2}
{\left|q_i/E_3+(q_i-p\cos\theta)/E_4\right|}.
$$

The two formulas describe the same observable. Their different energy and $2\pi$ factors live entirely in the normalization assigned to the external states and amplitude.

<h2 id="interpretation">Interpretation and common traps</h2>

The invariant master formula was not tied to the center-of-mass frame. What changes from frame to frame is the convenient parametrization of the final phase space. In the laboratory, a fixed outgoing direction does not fix the phase-space weight until recoil is included.

Common traps are:

1. Replacing the invariant flux with $4E_1m_2$ and forgetting the relative velocity.
2. Dropping the derivative of the energy constraint when integrating over $q$.
3. Using the center-of-mass result with lab values of $p$ and $q$.
4. Forgetting to sum over multiple physical roots when the kinematics permits them.
5. Mixing the source's delta-normalized amplitude with the notebook's covariant phase-space formula.

<h2 id="checkpoint">Checkpoint</h2>

The derivation has three indispensable ingredients:

1. the target-rest-frame flux $4m_2p$;
2. the recoil relation $\mathbf p_4=\mathbf p-\mathbf q$;
3. the Jacobian $|q/E_3+(q-p\cos\theta)/E_4|^{-1}$.

Once these are present, the result follows directly from invariant phase space without borrowing any center-of-mass formula.
