<h2 id="definition">1. Write the projection coefficient clearly</h2>

For two nonidentical particles in channel $n$, let

$$
|\mathbf p_1\sigma_1,\mathbf p_2\sigma_2;n\rangle
$$

be the product basis. In the source's normalization its scalar product contains ordinary three-momentum delta functions. Define the partial-wave state through

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\langle
\mathbf p_1\sigma_1,\mathbf p_2\sigma_2;n'
|E\mathbf p;j\sigma ls;n\rangle
\\
&\quad=
\left(\frac{kE_1E_2}{E}\right)^{-1/2}
\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf p_1-\mathbf p_2)
\delta(E-E_1-E_2)
\delta_{n'n}
\\
&\qquad\times
\sum_{m,\mu}
C_{s_1s_2}(s,\mu;\sigma_1,\sigma_2)
C_{ls}(j,\sigma;m,\mu)
Y_l^m(\widehat{\mathbf p}_1).
\end{aligned}
$$

In the center-of-mass frame,

$$
\mathbf p=0,
\qquad
\mathbf p_2=-\mathbf p_1,
\qquad
k=|\mathbf p_1|,
$$

and

$$
E_r(k)=\sqrt{k^2+M_r^2},
\qquad
E=E_1+E_2.
$$

The first Clebsch-Gordan coefficient couples the constituent spins to total spin $s,\mu$. The second couples $s,\mu$ and $l,m$ to total angular momentum $j,\sigma$.

<Callout title="What must be proved">
Momentum conservation, energy normalization, spherical-harmonic orthogonality, and angular-momentum coupling should each produce one delta symbol. The prefactor is correct only if no unwanted function of $k$, $E_1$, or $E_2$ survives.
</Callout>

<h2 id="completeness">2. Insert the product basis</h2>

Consider one state with arbitrary total momentum $\mathbf p'$ and one in the center-of-mass frame:

$$
\langle
E'\mathbf p';j'\sigma'l's';n'
|E\mathbf0;j\sigma ls;n
\rangle.
$$

Insert the product-state identity between them. Schematically,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\langle
E'\mathbf p';j'\sigma'l's';n'
|E\mathbf0;j\sigma ls;n
\rangle
\\
&\quad=
\sum_{\sigma_1,\sigma_2,n''}
\int d^3p_1\,d^3p_2\,
A_{E'\mathbf p';j'\sigma'l's'n'}^*
A_{E\mathbf0;j\sigma lsn},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $A$ denotes the projection coefficient written above. We now simplify its continuous and discrete pieces separately.

<h2 id="delta-functions">3. Collapse total momentum and total energy</h2>

The two momentum delta functions satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\delta^3(\mathbf p'-\mathbf p_1-\mathbf p_2)
\delta^3(\mathbf p_1+\mathbf p_2)
\\
&\qquad=
\delta^3(\mathbf p')
\delta^3(\mathbf p_1+\mathbf p_2).
\end{aligned}
$$

The $d^3p_2$ integration therefore sets

$$
\mathbf p_2=-\mathbf p_1
$$

and leaves the desired factor $\delta^3(\mathbf p')$.

Likewise,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\delta(E'-E_1-E_2)
\delta(E-E_1-E_2)
\\
&\qquad=
\delta(E'-E)
\delta(E-E_1-E_2).
\end{aligned}
$$

This produces the target factor $\delta(E'-E)$, but the second delta function still has to perform the radial momentum integral.

<h2 id="radial">4. Let the radial Jacobian cancel the prefactor</h2>

Use spherical coordinates for $\mathbf p_1$:

$$
d^3p_1=k^2\,dk\,d\Omega.
$$

Differentiate the two-particle energy:

$$
\frac{d(E_1+E_2)}{dk}
=
\frac{k}{E_1}+\frac{k}{E_2}
=
\frac{k(E_1+E_2)}{E_1E_2}.
$$

On the support of the energy delta function, $E_1+E_2=E$, so

$$
\boxed{
\delta(E-E_1-E_2)
=
\frac{E_1E_2}{kE}\,
\delta(k-k_E),
}
$$

where $k_E$ is the positive center-of-mass momentum at energy $E$.

The two state coefficients each contribute a square-root normalization. Once $E'=E$, their product is

$$
\left(\frac{kE_1E_2}{E}\right)^{-1}.
$$

The complete radial factor is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^\infty k^2dk\,
\left(\frac{kE_1E_2}{E}\right)^{-1}
\frac{E_1E_2}{kE}
\delta(k-k_E)
\\
&\qquad=
\int_0^\infty dk\,\delta(k-k_E)
=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the heart of the exercise:

$$
\boxed{
k^2
\times
\frac{E}{kE_1E_2}
\times
\frac{E_1E_2}{kE}
=1.
}
$$

No momentum- or energy-dependent factor survives.

<h2 id="angular-spin">5. Collapse the angular and spin labels</h2>

The angular integral gives

$$
\int d\Omega\,
Y_{l'}^{m'*}(\widehat{\mathbf k})
Y_l^m(\widehat{\mathbf k})
=
\delta_{l'l}\delta_{m'm}.
$$

The first spin-coupling coefficients obey

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\sigma_1,\sigma_2}
C_{s_1s_2}(s',\mu';\sigma_1,\sigma_2)^*
C_{s_1s_2}(s,\mu;\sigma_1,\sigma_2)
\\
&\qquad=
\delta_{s's}\delta_{\mu'\mu}.
\end{aligned}
$$

After the $l,m,s,\mu$ labels have been matched, the second coupling obeys

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{m,\mu}
C_{ls}(j',\sigma';m,\mu)^*
C_{ls}(j,\sigma;m,\mu)
\\
&\qquad=
\delta_{j'j}\delta_{\sigma'\sigma}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the channel labels supply $\delta_{n'n}$. Combining the continuous and discrete factors yields

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\langle
E'\mathbf p';j'\sigma'l's';n'
|E\mathbf0;j\sigma ls;n
\rangle
\\
&\quad=
\delta^3(\mathbf p')\delta(E'-E)
\delta_{j'j}\delta_{\sigma'\sigma}
\delta_{l'l}\delta_{s's}\delta_{n'n}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

For two arbitrary total momenta, the first delta function is

$$
\delta^3(\mathbf p'-\mathbf p).
$$

<h2 id="identical">6. What changes for identical particles?</h2>

For nonidentical particles, $(\mathbf k,-\mathbf k)$ labels a different ordered product state from $(-\mathbf k,\mathbf k)$. For identical particles those two descriptions differ only by exchange.

If one avoids double counting by integrating over only half of relative-momentum space, the angular domain is halved. The projection coefficient must then acquire an extra factor

$$
\boxed{\sqrt2}
$$

so that its squared magnitude compensates for the missing half of the domain.

The state must also obey the appropriate Bose or Fermi exchange condition. That condition removes incompatible combinations of orbital and spin quantum numbers; the normalization factor alone does not impose statistics.

<Callout title="Covariant normalization">
The source begins with product states normalized by ordinary $\delta^3$ functions. In the notebook's covariant basis, each external momentum state carries $2E(2\pi)^3$. Those factors migrate between the product-state completeness relation and the projection coefficient, but the invariant content is unchanged: the energy-normalized radial factor is the inverse square root of the two-body density-of-states Jacobian.
</Callout>

<div className="answer-check">
  <strong>Answer check</strong>
  <p>
    After all integrations, no factor of $k$, $E$, $E_1$, or $E_2$ remains. The result contains exactly one delta function for each continuous total label and one Kronecker delta for each discrete angular-momentum, spin, and channel label. That is the required normalization.
  </p>
</div>

<h2 id="checkpoint">Checkpoint</h2>

The normalization succeeds because three factors cancel:

$$
\underbrace{k^2dk}_{\text{radial measure}}
\times
\underbrace{\left(\frac{kE_1E_2}{E}\right)^{-1}}_{\text{two state coefficients}}
\times
\underbrace{\frac{E_1E_2}{kE}}_{\text{energy-delta Jacobian}}.
$$

Spherical harmonics then fix $l,m$, the first Clebsch-Gordan relation fixes $s,\mu$, and the second fixes $j,\sigma$.
