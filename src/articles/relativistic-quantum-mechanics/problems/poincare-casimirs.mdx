<h2 id="momentum">1. Momentum squared is invariant</h2>

Finite Lorentz transformations act as

$$
U(\Lambda)^{-1}P^\mu U(\Lambda)
=
\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}P^\nu.
$$

Translations commute with all momentum components, so they leave $P^\mu$ unchanged. Under the Lorentz part,

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(\Lambda)^{-1}P^2U(\Lambda)
&=
\eta_{\mu\nu}
\Lambda^\mu{}_{\rho}P^\rho
\Lambda^\nu{}_{\sigma}P^\sigma\\
&=
\left(\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda\right)_{\rho\sigma}
P^\rho P^\sigma\\
&=
\eta_{\rho\sigma}P^\rho P^\sigma\\
&=P^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
U(\Lambda,a)^{-1}P^2U(\Lambda,a)=P^2.
}
$$

Multiplying by $U(\Lambda,a)$ on the appropriate side gives

$$
\boxed{[P^2,U(\Lambda,a)]=0.}
$$

The calculation is the operator version of the elementary four-vector identity $(\Lambda p)^2=p^2$.

<h2 id="translations">2. The Pauli-Lubanski vector forgets the origin</h2>

Angular momentum depends on the spacetime origin. With the conjugation order used in this notebook,

$$
U(a)^{-1}M^{\rho\sigma}U(a)
=
M^{\rho\sigma}-a^\rho P^\sigma+a^\sigma P^\rho.
$$

Insert this into $W^\mu$. The possible change is

$$
\Delta W^\mu
=
\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}P_\nu
\left(-a_\rho P_\sigma+a_\sigma P_\rho\right).
$$

Consider the first term. It contains

$$
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}P_\nu P_\sigma.
$$

The momentum components commute, so $P_\nu P_\sigma$ is symmetric under $\nu\leftrightarrow\sigma$. The Levi-Civita tensor is antisymmetric under the same exchange. Their contraction is zero. The second term vanishes for the same reason. Therefore

$$
\boxed{
U(a)^{-1}W^\mu U(a)=W^\mu.
}
$$

This is the essential cancellation. Although total angular momentum about an origin changes, the intrinsic information packaged in $W^\mu$ does not.

<Sidenote>
Geometrically, translating the origin adds an orbital bivector $a\wedge P$ to $M$. Wedging that term with another $P$ gives $P\wedge P=0$.
</Sidenote>

<h2 id="lorentz">3. The Pauli-Lubanski vector is axial</h2>

Under a proper Lorentz transformation, both $P_\nu$ and $M_{\rho\sigma}$ transform tensorially. The Levi-Civita tensor converts their contraction into a four-vector:

$$
U(\Lambda)^{-1}W^\mu U(\Lambda)
=
\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}W^\nu,
\qquad
\det\Lambda=+1.
$$

For an improper transformation, $W^\mu$ is an axial vector and acquires an extra determinant:

$$
U(\Lambda)^{-1}W^\mu U(\Lambda)
=
(\det\Lambda)\Lambda^\mu{}_{\nu}W^\nu.
$$

In either case,

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(\Lambda)^{-1}W^2U(\Lambda)
&=
(\det\Lambda)^2
\eta_{\mu\nu}
\Lambda^\mu{}_{\rho}W^\rho
\Lambda^\nu{}_{\sigma}W^\sigma\\
&=
\eta_{\rho\sigma}W^\rho W^\sigma\\
&=W^2,
\end{aligned}
$$

because $(\det\Lambda)^2=1$ and $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda=\eta$.

Translations already leave $W^\mu$ unchanged, so

$$
\boxed{
U(\Lambda,a)^{-1}W^2U(\Lambda,a)=W^2,
}
$$

and consequently

$$
\boxed{[W^2,U(\Lambda,a)]=0.}
$$

<h2 id="meaning">4. Why these invariants label a particle</h2>

A <Term>Casimir operator</Term> commutes with every element of the symmetry representation. By Schur's lemma, it acts as a fixed number on an irreducible representation.

For a massive positive-energy representation,

$$
P^2=m^2,
$$

$$
W^2=-m^2j(j+1).
$$

Thus the pair $(m,j)$ is not attached to one convenient frame. It labels the entire family of states connected by Poincaré transformations.

For an ordinary massless helicity representation,

$$
P^2=0,
\qquad
W^2=0,
\qquad
W^\mu=hP^\mu.
$$

The first two Casimirs do not distinguish different finite helicities because every null vector has zero norm. The proportionality between $W^\mu$ and $P^\mu$, together with the little-group representation, retains the helicity information. This is why the <Link to="/chapters/relativistic-quantum-mechanics/one-particle-states#little-group">little-group analysis</Link> is still necessary after the Casimirs are known.

<div className="answer-check">
  <strong>Rest-frame check</strong>
  <p>
    For a massive state at rest, $P^\mu=(m,\mathbf0)$ and $W^\mu=(0,m\mathbf J)$ up to the fixed index convention. Hence $W^2=-m^2\mathbf J^2=-m^2j(j+1)$, confirming both the sign and the physical interpretation.
  </p>
</div>

**Common trap:** proving only that $W^\mu$ transforms covariantly under Lorentz transformations. Because $M^{\mu\nu}$ changes under translations, translation invariance must be checked separately. The antisymmetric contraction is what removes the origin-dependent orbital part.

<h2 id="checkpoint">Checkpoint</h2>

Momentum transforms as a four-vector, while the Pauli-Lubanski vector is a translation-invariant axial four-vector. Their invariant contractions therefore satisfy

$$
\boxed{
[P^2,U(\Lambda,a)]=0,
\qquad
[W^2,U(\Lambda,a)]=0.
}
$$

These operators measure what no change of inertial frame or spacetime origin can alter: the mass and intrinsic spin content of a Poincaré representation.
