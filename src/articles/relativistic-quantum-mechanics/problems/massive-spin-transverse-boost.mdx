<h2 id="problem">Problem</h2>

<div className="problem-statement">
  <span className="problem-label">A massive vector particle seen from the side</span>
  <p>
    Observer $O$ describes a massive spin-one particle with momentum $p$ along $+y$ and canonical spin component $\sigma$ along $z$. A second observer $O'$ moves with velocity $v$ along $+z$ relative to $O$. What momentum and spin state does $O'$ assign to the particle?
  </p>
</div>

<details className="problem-hint">
  <summary>Hint</summary>
  <p>
    First boost the four-momentum. For the spin, compare the actual route $B_z(-\eta)L(p)$ with the chosen rotationless standard boost $L(p')$. Their difference leaves the rest momentum fixed, so it must be a rotation.
  </p>
</details>

<p>
  This is the concrete calculation behind <Link to="/chapters/relativistic-quantum-mechanics/one-particle-states#wigner-rotation">the Wigner-rotation discussion</Link>. We use covariantly normalized states, so no square-root energy factor accompanies the transformation.
</p>

<Callout title="Observer convention">
Observer $O'$ moves along $+z$, so components reported by $O'$ are obtained with the passive boost $B_z(-\eta)$, where $\tanh\eta=v$. An active boost in the opposite convention reverses the signs of the displayed rotation angles, but not the mixing probabilities.
</Callout>

<h2 id="momentum">1. Transform the momentum</h2>

Write

$$
p^\mu=(E,0,p,0),
\qquad
E=\sqrt{m^2+p^2}.
$$

Introduce the particle rapidity $\xi$ and the observer rapidity $\eta$:

$$
\cosh\xi=\frac Em,
\qquad
\sinh\xi=\frac pm,
\qquad
\tanh\eta=v,
\qquad
\cosh\eta=\gamma_v.
$$

The momentum measured by $O'$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
p'^\mu
&=B_z(-\eta)p^\mu\\
&=\boxed{(\gamma_vE,\,0,\,p,\,-\gamma_vvE)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The $y$ component is unchanged because the observer boost is along $z$. The negative $z$ component is ordinary aberration: an observer moving toward $+z$ sees the particle arrive with a component toward $-z$.

The mass shell is an immediate check:

$$
(p'^0)^2-|\mathbf p'|^2
=
\gamma_v^2E^2-p^2-\gamma_v^2v^2E^2
=E^2-p^2=m^2.
$$

<h2 id="wigner">2. Isolate the Wigner rotation</h2>

The initial canonical spin state is defined by a rotationless boost from rest,

$$
L(p)=B_y(\xi).
$$

After the observer boost, let $L(p')$ denote the rotationless boost that takes $(m,\mathbf0)$ directly to $p'$. Comparing the two paths gives

$$
W
=
L^{-1}(p')B_z(-\eta)B_y(\xi).
$$

Acting on the rest momentum,

$$
W(m,\mathbf0)
=
L^{-1}(p')p'
=(m,\mathbf0),
$$

so $W$ belongs to the massive little group. Because the two boosts lie in the $yz$ plane, their residual rotation is about the perpendicular $x$-axis:

$$
\boxed{W=R_x(-\Omega)}.
$$

Multiplying the boosts gives

$$
\sin\Omega
=
\frac{\sinh\eta\sinh\xi}
{\cosh\eta\cosh\xi+1}
=
\frac{\gamma_vvp}{\gamma_vE+m},
$$

$$
\cos\Omega
=
\frac{\cosh\eta+\cosh\xi}
{\cosh\eta\cosh\xi+1}
=
\frac{\gamma_vm+E}{\gamma_vE+m}.
$$

The most compact form is

$$
\boxed{
\tan\Omega
=
\frac{\gamma_vvp}{\gamma_vm+E}
}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\tan\frac\Omega2
=
\tanh\frac\eta2\tanh\frac\xi2.
$$

This is the finite form of the algebraic statement

$$
[K_y,K_z]=-iJ_x.
$$

Two non-collinear boosts cannot combine into only another boost.

<h2 id="spin-one">3. Let the rotation act on spin one</h2>

For covariantly normalized canonical states,

$$
\boxed{
U\!\left(B_z(-\eta)\right)|p,\sigma\rangle
=
\sum_{\sigma'=-1}^{1}
D^{(1)}_{\sigma'\sigma}\!\left(R_x(-\Omega)\right)
|p',\sigma'\rangle.
}
$$

In the ordered basis $(+1,0,-1)$,

$$
D^{(1)}\!\left(R_x(-\Omega)\right)
=e^{+i\Omega J_x}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{1+\cos\Omega}{2}
&\dfrac{i\sin\Omega}{\sqrt2}
&-\dfrac{1-\cos\Omega}{2}\\[7pt]
\dfrac{i\sin\Omega}{\sqrt2}
&\cos\Omega
&\dfrac{i\sin\Omega}{\sqrt2}\\[7pt]
-\dfrac{1-\cos\Omega}{2}
&\dfrac{i\sin\Omega}{\sqrt2}
&\dfrac{1+\cos\Omega}{2}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For example, if $O$ prepared the state with $\sigma=0$, then $O'$ writes it as

$$
|p,0\rangle
\longrightarrow
\frac{i\sin\Omega}{\sqrt2}|p',+1\rangle
+\cos\Omega|p',0\rangle
+\frac{i\sin\Omega}{\sqrt2}|p',-1\rangle.
$$

The corresponding probabilities are

$$
P(0\to0)=\cos^2\Omega,
\qquad
P(0\to\pm1)=\frac12\sin^2\Omega.
$$

<Sidenote>
Momentum-dependent phase conventions for the spin basis can move the factors of $i$ around. The rotation angle and every probability are convention independent.
</Sidenote>

<h2 id="checks">Checks and interpretation</h2>

<div className="answer-check">
  <strong>Three limiting checks</strong>
  <p>
    If $v=0$, if $p=0$, or if the two boosts are collinear, then $\Omega=0$. In every case where there is no non-collinear pair of boosts, the spin mixing disappears exactly as it should.
  </p>
</div>

For small particle and observer speeds,

$$
\Omega\simeq\frac12uv,
\qquad
u=\frac pE,
$$

which is the infinitesimal Thomas-Wigner rotation. The factor of one half is a useful diagnostic; simply rotating by the change in the momentum direction would give the wrong answer.

The particle remains a spin-one particle. What changes is the component assigned relative to the canonical axes used at the new momentum. Spin is not a little arrow that every observer boosts independently of the momentum.

**Common trap:** transforming only $p^\mu$ and carrying the label $\sigma$ across unchanged. That would ignore the little-group element $L^{-1}(p')\Lambda L(p)$, which is precisely where relativistic spin lives.

<h2 id="checkpoint">Checkpoint</h2>

The complete observer change is

$$
\boxed{
p'^\mu=(\gamma_vE,0,p,-\gamma_vvE),
\qquad
|p,\sigma\rangle
\mapsto
D^{(1)}(R_x(-\Omega))|p',\sigma\rangle,
}
$$

with

$$
\boxed{
\tan\Omega=\frac{\gamma_vvp}{\gamma_vm+E}.
}
$$

The momentum change is a Lorentz boost; the spin change is the Wigner rotation left over after the final standard boost is removed.
